Design Specification (15%)

# Introduction

Your group should come up with a web application to develop. Let your imagination fly but be practical! We will mainly mark the quality of the design and implementation and only reserve very few marks for the novelty of the idea.

The application you design needs to:

* involve user authentication;
* interact with some kind of model stored in a database;
* be visually appealing and have an intuitive user interface;
* allow the user to input something which is then used in some way by the application.
* overall, the functionality supported should be rich enough in order to allow you to demonstrate an understanding of the technologies covered in this course.

Having discussed your ideas within your team, you are required to come up with a Design Specification, which will provide a whole range of details regarding the design of the web application that you intend to implement. This will include an overview, a specification, system architecture diagram, ER diagram, site map, and wireframes.

The Design Specification is worth 15% of the overall assessment of the course and is due in by **12 February at 4.30pm**. One submission per team is required.

# What should be included in the submission

Imagine that you are submitting your idea to get funding – so you have to prepare a Powerpoint presentation to describe:

1. An **overview** of the application (i.e., what it does and why it is useful) – 1 slide.
   *Your overview should clearly state the intended users, the problem the application addresses, and the value it provides.*
2. The **specification** as prioritised user stories (MoSCoW) – 1 slide.
   *Your specification should provide 6-10 user stories in the form: “As a…, I want…, so that…” and label each story as Must / Should / Could / Won’t (e.g. M1, M2, .., S1, S2, ...).*

*Your set of Must requirements must include user authentication, interaction with a database-backed model, and at least one case where the user inputs data which is saved and used by the application.*

1. A high-level **system architecture diagram** – 1 slide.
   *This diagram should show the main components of your system, and any external APIs/services (if applicable).*
2. An **ER Diagram** (in compressed Chen notation), along with a description of the attributes in each entity – 2 slides max.
   *The model should be clearly relevant to your specification and should reflect relationships and constraints appropriately.*
3. A **site map** showing the site organisation – 1 slide.
   *You must also label pages with the Must requirements they support.*
4. A number of **wireframes** to show the main functionality of the system – at least 2 and at most 5 slides.

*Your wireframes should illustrate the user interface for the core functionality of the system and must cover all Must requirements.*

1. An **accessibility plan** – 1 slide.

Your plan should list five web content accessibility guidelines ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/)) you will consider in your design, with a short phrase describing what it means for your web application. Your five criteria may relate to keyboard/focus, forms/errors, structure/navigation, colour/contrast, feedback/status messages.

**Appendix** – 1 slide. Your appendix should specify **each team member’s contribution** to this coursework (in %) and what they contributed to it. Your appendix should also include your **AI use statement**.

# Working in a team

You should think about how the tasks associated with the design of your team’s web app should be divided. Try to ensure that activities are assigned so that every member of the team can be involved at all times.

Please note that under normal circumstances all members in a team will be assigned the same mark.

Teamwork is an important part of software development: in industry, large software development tasks are constantly being undertaken in teams that can vary in size from two to several hundred. In any team it is recognised that people will contribute in different ways. It is important to ensure that you are always aware of your role and that you have an opportunity to make a meaningful contribution to the project at all times.

All members of a team will usually receive the same mark. Where members of a team find the contribution of other members to be unsatisfactory, you are encouraged to resolve the issue internally by negotiation first. However, if this is not possible, the course coordinator should be asked to intervene as soon as possible.

The mark awarded to individual members of the team may be adjusted to reflect the individual’s contribution (i.e. if you do not contribute at all, your group coursework mark will be set to 0). **We will be using Deltas as a way of adjusting the team mark in order to arrive at an individual’s mark for the team-based components of the course (Design Specification, Presentation and Project).**

Each member of a team will be required to submit a form, giving their own as well as their team members’ contributions. A Delta typically adjusts the team mark up or down by 0 or more bands for a given individual according to the contribution. The computation of these Deltas will be informed by the percentage scores that each member of the team will provide, which gives a numerical estimate of the proportion of the overall effort undertaken by each person (including themselves). We will also look at the contributions listed in the appendix to validate any adjustments.

# Generative AI (GenAI) use in this assessment

You may use Generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini) to support your work. However, if you use GenAI for anything beyond general background reading, you must **acknowledge how you used it** and **attribute it appropriately**. You remain responsible for the accuracy and originality of our submission.

## When you must acknowledge GenAI use

If you use GenAI to generate content that influences your submission (e.g., app ideas, user stories, WCAG criteria choices, diagram structure, slide wording), you must include an **AI Use Statement** (see below). Where GenAI provides specific claims or facts, you should verify them using reliable sources.

## Examples

1. **Brainstorming**
   You ask GenAI to suggest app ideas or features (e.g., “Suggest web app ideas suitable for this course” or “Generate possible user stories for a habit tracker”). You should acknowledge that GenAI was used for brainstorming and briefly describe what you took from it.
2. **Drafting or refining user stories / wording**
   You ask GenAI to rewrite user stories, improve clarity, or propose MoSCoW priorities. You should acknowledge GenAI use and ensure the final stories reflect our intent and course scope.
3. **Selecting accessibility criteria**
   You ask GenAI to propose WCAG 2.2 success criteria relevant to your app. You should acknowledge this and ensure the criteria listed are correct and relevant (you should still consult the WCAG Quick Reference directly).
4. **Generating diagrams or structure**
   You ask GenAI to propose an ER model, sitemap structure, or architecture diagram. You should acknowledge the use and ensure the final diagrams are coherent, consistent, and reflect our application accurately.

## What is not acceptable

* Submitting GenAI-generated content **without acknowledgement**.
* Including “facts” from GenAI without checking reliability where accuracy matters.
* Presenting GenAI output as if it were an authoritative external source.

The university position is that using GenAI in assessments without acknowledgement may constitute academic misconduct (plagiarism).

## AI Use Statement (required if GenAI used beyond background reading)

In your appendix slide, you must include an **“AI Use Statement”** and include any of the following statements that apply:

**Declaration on the use of Generative AI:**

1. We declare that we have not used GenAI in the development of this submission.
2. We declare that we have used GenAI as background reading only.
3. We declare that we have used GenAI for copy-editing / improving clarity of language.
4. We declare that we have used GenAI to brainstorm ideas (e.g., app concept/features/user stories).
5. We declare that we have used GenAI to help draft or refine requirements/user stories (including MoSCoW).
6. We declare that we have used GenAI to help identify relevant WCAG 2.2 success criteria for the accessibility slide.
7. We declare that we have used GenAI to help generate or refine diagrams (architecture / ER / sitemap / wireframes).
8. We declare that we have used GenAI in another way (briefly explain).
